# MenuButton trigger state — tooltip, pressed paint, alignment

Three fixes to the `MenuButton` trigger, all of them about what the trigger looks like while the popup it owns is open, and one about how the trigger's copy sits inside a pinned width. Reported from the model-picker pattern (ghost variant, 320 px): the tooltip was still floating over the open rows, the ghost trigger had dropped back to its rest fill so the control looked switched off, and the caret had come to rest beside the text instead of on the right edge.

**The tooltip stands down while its own surface is open.** Suppression is structural, at the `Menu` level, not a prop the caller has to remember. `Menu` already stamps `aria-expanded` on whatever element it is handed as a trigger; it now also stamps `data-menu-open` while open — a data attribute rather than a React-only prop, because a plain `<button>` trigger would paste an unknown prop onto the DOM. `TooltipWrapper`, the one tip implementation behind both `Tooltip` and `Button`'s `tooltip`, checks that marker on its anchor (and inside it, for the `<Tooltip>` case, which wraps its children in a span) at two moments: when the delay elapses, and continuously while visible, through a `MutationObserver` on the anchor. A tip that was already up when the menu opened comes down; a tip still waiting out its delay never appears. `MenuButton`'s existing `tooltipDisabled={menuOpen || …}` mirror stays as belt-and-braces — it is the cheap path, the observer is the one that catches the hand-wired case the prop cannot reach.

**An open trigger keeps the paint it had on hover.** A trigger whose popup is open is the pressed control, so it must not lose its fill the moment the pointer travels to the rows. Each variant gets its open paint derived from its own hover token — `getButtonOpenClasses` rewrites `hover:` to `aria-expanded:` across the whole variant/colour table, so there is no second colour table to drift out of sync, and the rule rides the disclosure attribute rather than a component state, which is why `DropdownButton`'s caret inherits it for free. `aria-expanded` is Tailwind-native (`[aria-expanded="true"]`), so no CSS was authored. The paint is skipped when `active` is set — that prop already owns the on-state story — and for `accent` and glass, which build their own fill. `themeSafelist.test.ts` walks the derived classes for all 8 variants × 21 tones against the built stylesheet, and `scripts/generate-safelist.mjs` gained the `aria-expanded:` shapes it demands: template-literal classes are invisible to the scanner, and a missing shape ships as a class name that paints nothing.

**The caret is always on the right edge; `align` moves the copy.** A new `align?: "left" | "center" | "right"` (default `left`) sets how the stacked title and subtitle sit inside the trigger. Only the copy moves: the text column becomes the flexible child (`flex-1 min-w-0`) so the caret — and a trailing pill, which now carries `shrink-0` in both its raw-node and config forms — hold the right edge whatever the alignment. That flex ownership is the fix for the caret hugging the text: `Button` centres its slots, so without a flexible column the whole group parks in the middle and the chevron lands next to the words. Alignment needs room to read, which is what `width` / `maxWidth` give it.

Verified by unit tests in all three areas (Button's held paint across variants and tones, and its absence when closed/active/accent/glass; MenuButton's caret placement, `aria-expanded`/`data-menu-open` stamping and open fill; tooltip suppression for both a hand-wrapped `Tooltip` and one still pending when the menu opens), by the safelist walk, and by the compiled selectors in `react/dist/index.css` — the consumer-facing artifact here is that stylesheet, so the kit must be rebuilt for any of this to appear in an app that imports it.
